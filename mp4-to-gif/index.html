Convert MP4 to GIF without uploading your video

Turn an MP4 video into a GIF for a tutorial, product demo or message. Adjust the frame rate and width, then preview and download the animation. Conversion runs in your browser, and your source video stays on your device during processing.

How to convert your MP4

Choose a supported MP4 file from your device. Review the quality mode, FPS and width settings before starting. Allow the converter to load its engine, then wait for processing to finish. Inspect the generated animation and use Download to save the output. Keep your source video until you have opened the GIF in the application where you intend to share it.

For a first attempt, use a short clip that shows one clear action. Screen recordings of a menu opening, a small product interaction or a simple demonstration are easier to evaluate than a long movie. If selecting several files, check each output individually. Different sources can produce different results even with the same settings.

Choose width and frame rate for the destination

Width controls the output dimensions. A smaller width usually needs less data but can make small interface labels harder to read. Choose a size suitable for the place where the GIF will appear. Previewing a small animation stretched across a large display can make it look softer than it will at its intended size.

FPS means frames per second. More frames can make motion appear smoother, while fewer frames can reduce the amount of data. For example, 10 FPS describes ten images per second, while 20 FPS describes twenty. These numbers explain the setting; they do not predict the final file size. Source movement, colors, duration and the selected quality mode also matter.

Make a smaller GIF without hiding the important action

Try reducing the width first if the GIF will be viewed in a small message or document. Then lower the frame rate and compare the motion. Change one setting at a time so you can see the tradeoff. Use a shorter source clip if only a few seconds are relevant, and preserve enough frames to make the action understandable.

Fine text, gradients and rapid movement can be difficult to represent in GIF. Quality modes with palette optimization can improve how the available colors are used, but may take more processing. Review the result against your source. There is no single setting that guarantees the smallest file and best appearance for every video.

Local processing and first-run loading

The conversion engine runs locally through WebAssembly. The page may download engine files before processing begins, which can make the first run slower to start. This download brings software to your browser; it does not send your selected MP4 to a conversion server. The processing status helps distinguish loading from conversion.

Processing time depends on video length, dimensions, settings and available device memory. Keep the page open while it works. If a job fails, try a smaller supported source and lower output settings. Changing a filename extension does not change the video's actual encoding. A file can have an MP4 extension while containing a stream that the engine cannot read.

Common questions

Why is my GIF larger than the original MP4?

MP4 video codecs can represent motion much more efficiently than GIF. A GIF therefore may grow even when its dimensions are smaller. Compare a shorter clip, lower width and fewer frames. Keep MP4 when compact video playback is more important than a GIF-compatible animation.

Does the GIF include audio?

GIF has no audio track. Use the original video when speech, music or other sound is part of the explanation. Converting to GIF does not preserve that audio in the downloaded animation.

Is this a GPU converter?

This tool uses a local FFmpeg WebAssembly engine. It does not offer a GPU processing mode. Hardware options on other video tools do not imply that this GIF converter uses the same path.

Can I use a private recording?

The conversion path processes the selected recording locally. Check your own handling requirements before choosing a file. Website resources and any analytics requests are separate from file conversion and are described in the privacy policy.
